The chat is not the map.

The window you are looking at is a projection. The record underneath it is the thing you can still open later. Scroll is the closest academic statement of that split we have found: an append-only event log as lossless ground truth, and a working view that is allowed to forget without destroying the addresses.

Our read

A window you cannot rebuild is rent.

A company brain that only exists as the current context window is a filing cabinet you empty every night. Corrections die. Yesterday’s “changed” is gone. The next run re-derives, or worse, re-invents.

Scroll’s useful object is not the Python kernel. It is the split: lossless log underneath, working view on top. When the view nears its budget, stale spans are evicted and stay recoverable at exact addresses. The chat is allowed to be lossy because the log is not.

We do not install Scroll. We do not run their kernel. Our object is Facts with receipts on a graph you own — source, date, confidence, last-checked — not a sandboxed interpreter per session. “The chat is not the map” is our sentence for that split, not theirs. The rhyme is the ground-truth layer. A projection you can rebuild is an asset. A window you cannot rebuild is rent.

The design sentence is load-bearing. Author-reported benchmark scores are not, until we reproduce them — and we have not. They stay off this page.

Fence

Scroll is a preprint (21 August 2026).

This page is the design split, not a scoreboard. We did not re-run the authors’ LongMemEval or BEAM numbers. Per-session kernel cost is an open question the paper does not close. Do not read this page as a Mnemma benchmark, or as a claim that we ship Scroll.
