Notes · August 2026 · 01

Everyone gains with AI in the short run. The long run depends on how you engage.

A framework making the rounds this month puts a name on something every AI-using team has felt: the speed is real, and so is the slow erosion underneath it. Our read: the fix isn't personal discipline. It's structure.

The framework

Eight modes, one gradient: who's doing the thinking?

The 8 Modes of AI Engagement — a framework from Mark Keith, professor of Information Systems at BYU, amplified this month by Brian Roemmele — classifies how people work with AI on a gradient of cognitive agency, from near-total passivity to full human control over what the problem even is. Receipt SourceMark Keith, PhD (BYU Information Systems), aimodes.ai — research foundations; amplified by @BrianRoemmele, 2026-08-18. ConfidenceThe taxonomy is theoretically derived — grounded in Bloom, Chi's ICAP, Zimmerman, Vygotsky, Schön — and, by the site's own statement, "not empirically validated through factor analysis." Not a peer-reviewed paper; we quote it at that confidence, not above it. Supporting studiesBastani et al., Generative AI can harm learning (SSRN 4895486); Doshi & Hauser, Science Advances 10(28), 2024 (doi:10.1126/sciadv.adn5290).

The eight modes group into three tiers:

  • Passivity (1–2): Oracle, Production. Ask, accept, ship. The AI does the cognitive work; you do the pasting.
  • Partnership (3–4): Tutor, Collaborative Problem-Solver. Shared effort — you supply context, direct the iteration, stay engaged enough to learn.
  • Agency (5–8): Verification Agent, Critical Challenger, Creative Expander, Problem Setter. You check the claims, argue with the reasoning, push past the given frame — and at the top, you decide what the problem actually is.

The central pattern: AI assistance boosts immediate performance for nearly everyone. Take the AI away, and people who lived in the passive modes tend to land below people who never used it. People who verified, challenged, and framed problems keep their gains. Receipt Evidence shapeBastani et al. ran ~1,000 high-school students: large short-term gains with AI, performance drop without it afterward — except in a tutor-scaffolded arm. Doshi & Hauser: individual creativity up, collective diversity of output down. Honest caveatsThe widely-shared "90% negative transfer" split is the site's own approximation ("varies across studies and task types"), and the viral chart is labeled a conceptual synthesis — "trajectories are illustrative." Directionally supported; not a measured universal.

The replies were the good part

Practitioners already work this way

The conversation under the post is better product research than the chart. The people getting durable value from AI describe the same moves, in their own words:

  • The modes are tools, not a moral ladder. "Lighter ones for quick work. Problem Setter only when the job calls for it." — @mrdanielbar
  • Problem framing is the thing to protect. "AI can give you a great answer to the wrong question and make it feel productive. The human still has to decide what problem is actually worth solving." — @DanielNguyenHQ
  • Bind the problem before you hand it over. "Binding it to a certain problem and range of possibilities really helps it dial in quickly. It really does not handle open ended things very well." — @badbirb, a CTO on working in Problem Setter mode
  • Verification has to be wired in, not hoped for. "Mode 5 (Verification Agent) is where agent systems break most. Fluent 'done' is Oracle/Production… Agency is a ladder you encode — not a vibe check." — @AbuZ8Studios, on builder-≠-judge gates and disk receipts

That last one is the sentence we'd frame. Because it points at the part of this conversation almost everyone skips: what happens when it's not one person choosing a mode — it's a company.

The part that concerns a business

Individual discipline doesn't survive a deadline

For one person, mode choice is a habit you can practice. For a company of forty, it's a statistical certainty that under pressure, work slides to the cheapest mode available. Oracle and Production are the cheapest modes. Prompt, paste, ship — it feels productive, and every instance of it is locally rational.

The long-run cost doesn't land on the person who pasted. It lands on the company:

  • Answers that nobody verified become decisions that nobody can audit. Six months later, "why did we do this?" has no answer that survives checking.
  • The re-research loop quietly eats the speed gain — every fluent answer that can't be trusted gets re-derived by the next person who needs it.
  • The "great answer to the wrong question" ships, because problem framing was left to whoever was in the chat window that day.

Telling a team "engage in the high-agency modes" is a poster on a wall. Under load, culture loses to convenience. If you want verification, challenge, and problem framing to actually happen, they have to be properties of the system, not resolutions of the staff.

Our read

Encode the high-agency modes. That's the whole design.

This is the argument Mnemma is built on, arrived at from the other direction. We didn't start from a learning-science taxonomy — we started from the observation that unaudited AI answers rot a company's knowledge. But the mapping is one-to-one:

  • Verification (Mode 5), structural. Every important fact in a Mnemma brain carries a receipt — source, date, confidence, last checked — so "is that actually true?" is one click, not a re-research project. And the builder is never the judge: proposed facts only enter the map through a separate adjudication step.   • Challenge (Mode 6), with the map on the table. You can only argue productively with an answer that shows its sources. A receipted brain turns "I disagree" from a status contest into a check: follow the receipt, find the weak link or concede the point.
  • Problem setting (Mode 8), kept human on purpose. The brain does the remembering so your people spend their attention deciding what matters — not re-finding context for the fifth time. The framework's authors put it well: the scarce resource isn't access to intelligence, it's the capacity to direct, evaluate, and reframe it.

And here is the inversion worth noticing: once the oracle is auditable, the passive modes stop being dangerous. Oracle mode against a black box trains your team to stop checking. Oracle mode against a receipted brain is just a fast answer with its provenance attached — the check is one click away on the day the answer matters. You keep the short-run speed everyone gets; the structure keeps the long-run trust that most teams lose.

Sharper or duller isn't decided by which model you buy. It's decided by whether verification, challenge, and problem framing are wired into the system your company thinks with. That's a thing you can install.

Start by measuring the estate, not hoping for better habits.
