Why receipts

We made the whole category checkable, starting with ourselves.

We scanned this category twice, adversarially, and found that vendor claims routinely could not be verified — even under checking designed to disprove our own conclusion. Receipt MethodTwo-round competitive scan; second round tried to refute the first. FindingNo vendor offered all five of: runs in your environment · you own the graph · facts carry provenance · products operate on top · mid-market pricing. Source/brain/pricing-mechanics#claim-category-scan

So we decided the fix had to be structural, not rhetorical. Anyone can write "trusted" on a page. We built trust into the shape of the thing instead:

  • Receipts on the product's facts — source, date, confidence, last-checked, on every important claim in your brain.
  • Receipts on the quote's math — every number comes from a formula, with its inputs shown.
  • Receipts on this website's claims — every factual statement here carries the same footnote you just clicked.

It's the one thing a competitor can't copy from a screenshot: you can't fake a receipt discipline you don't have. The click either resolves to something real or it doesn't.

Week six of the unreceipted analyst

The market is teaching itself to build memory-agents. Without receipts.

A new genre is spreading: a persistent “analyst” that watches sources, keeps a current view, and wakes the team with a brief. Week one looks magical. Week six is where the pattern fails — not because the model got worse, because the memory was never an owned, checkable asset.

  • Drift. The “current view” silently diverges from the sources. Nobody can say when.
  • No provenance. A fluent line with no way to check it. Fluency gets mistaken for evidence.
  • Corrections die. A human fix in the chat does not survive the next run. The same error comes back tomorrow.
  • Nothing you keep. The memory lives in someone else’s cloud. Cancel, and the history walks.

We don’t name the tutorials. The pattern is the point. The differentiator is mechanism, not advice: containers that refuse unclassified data, source rules that rank what may be believed, an adjudicator who is never the builder (builder≠judge), and a graph you own.

Fluency is not evidence. A confident answer without a receipt is unverified — not a fact. Receipt · why this page exists twice PatternPublic how-tos now sell persistent-memory brief agents. The failure modes above are structural: no provenance, no owned graph, no adjudication, corrections that don’t persist.
